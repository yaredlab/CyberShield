Phishing Attacks

Phishing tricks users into giving away personal information through convincing fake emails, text messages, or websites. Attackers pretend to be trusted organizations — like your bank or a popular app.

Always verify the sender's address before clicking any link. Look for spelling errors, urgent language, or requests for personal information as warning signs.

Malware & Ransomware

Malware is harmful software designed to damage, disrupt, or steal your data. It can arrive through downloads, email attachments, or infected websites — often without your knowledge.

Ransomware is a type of malware that locks your files and demands payment to restore them. Keep your antivirus updated and avoid downloading files from unknown or untrusted sources.

Password Safety

Strong, unique passwords protect your accounts from hackers and data breaches. Use a mix of uppercase letters, lowercase letters, numbers, and symbols. Avoid obvious words like your name or birthday.

Never reuse the same password across multiple platforms. A password manager can help you generate and store strong passwords securely.

Cyber safety

In today's digital world, cyber safety is just as important as locking your house door before sleeping. The internet is full of useful information, entertainment, and communication, but it also contains dangers like hackers, scammers, viruses, and fake websites.

Cyber safety awareness means learning how to protect your personal information online. Use strong passwords with a mix of letters, numbers, and symbols, and avoid suspicious links and messages promising unexpected rewards.

Keeping devices updated is important because updates fix security problems. Antivirus software, two-factor authentication, and sensible privacy settings can help keep your accounts safe. Be cautious on public Wi‑Fi networks.

Cyber safety is for everyone — students, teachers, workers, and family members. Stay alert, think before you click, and follow basic safety rules to enjoy the online world securely.
