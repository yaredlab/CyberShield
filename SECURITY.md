# Security Policy & Vulnerability Disclosure

CyberShield is committed to upholding rigorous standards of security, privacy, and responsible disclosure. We appreciate the contributions of security researchers and community members who help protect our academic digital infrastructure.

---

## 🛡️ Supported Versions

We actively maintain and provide security patches for the following versions:

| Version | Supported          | Maintenance Status |
| :------ | :----------------- | :----------------- |
| 2.x.x   | :white_check_mark: | Active Support     |
| 1.x.x   | :x:                | End of Life (EOL)  |

---

## 📬 Reporting a Vulnerability

If you discover a security flaw, vulnerability, or sensitive data exposure, please notify our team directly before publishing details publicly.

- **Primary Contact**: [schemchy27@gmail.com](mailto:schemchy27@gmail.com)
- **Subject Header**: `[VULNERABILITY REPORT] CyberShield — <Brief Issue Description>`
- **Machine-Readable Declaration**: [`.well-known/security.txt`](.well-known/security.txt) (RFC 9116 compliant)

### Submission Checklist

To help us review and triage your submission efficiently, please provide:
1. **Description**: A clear summary of the potential vulnerability and its estimated impact.
2. **Steps to Reproduce**: A minimal, reproducible proof-of-concept (PoC) or step-by-step instructions.
3. **Target Environment**: Browser versions, operating system, and specific pages or scripts involved.
4. **Remediation Suggestion**: Any proposed code fix or configuration change (optional but appreciated).

---

## ⏱️ Response & Remediation Timelines

- **Initial Acknowledgment**: Within **48 hours** of receiving the report.
- **Triage & Assessment**: Within **5 business days** with status validation.
- **Remediation & Patch Release**: Critical vulnerabilities will be addressed promptly, with public credit attributed upon mutual release consent.

---

## 🚫 Out of Scope

The following testing activities are strictly prohibited:
- Denial of Service (DoS / DDoS) attacks against hosting infrastructure.
- Social engineering, phishing, or physical intrusion against project contributors or academic staff.
- Automated vulnerability scanners executing high-volume, disruptive requests.
